Epistemic Framework of the Consensus Manifold
The epistem engine is a computational framework designed to resolve conflicts and coordinate consensus across decentralized networks where no single node holds absolute epistemic authority, and where persistent disagreement is a structural property of the system.1 Rather than forcing negotiation into a cognitive monoculture through scalar optimization—which often discards minority positions in favor of a homogenized average—the engine models consensus over a high-dimensional coordinate system known as the consensus manifold.2 This framework draws upon Charles Sanders Peirce’s theory of inquiry, Wilfrid Sellars’ concept of the space of reasons, and active inference principles, viewing scientific and political agreement not as an objective, absolute truth-pointing mechanism, but as a rational consensus that survives systematic questioning within a community of inquiry.4

T1b & T1c: Profile Collisions and Semantic Warnings
When processing short, generic sentences, the sparse TF-IDF matrix lacks sufficient distinct vocabulary signals to establish separate semantic trajectories during SVD.3 Under these conditions, different documents collapse onto the exact same coordinates, producing identical profiles.3 The validation suite verifies that a UserWarning containing the phrase "identical profiles" is emitted when duplicate profiles are generated, ensuring silent profile collapses are avoided.3 Real-world validation confirms that while synthetic text fragments collapse, longer and highly specific texts (30-50 words typical of pharmaceutical, environmental, or policy documentation) provide enough SVD signal to prevent collisions.3
